# Founder

## The Story Behind Origami Vault

My name is Sócrates Medina, and Origami Vault began long before the project itself.

For most of my life, I have been passionate about creating things.

Technology has always fascinated me, but my true passion has been video games. Over the years I explored game development using tools such as GameMaker Studio, Unity, and later Godot. I also shared what I learned through my YouTube channel, PadreGamer, where I published tutorials about game development and software creation.

Even while working professionally in software and technology, I often found myself thinking about ideas I wanted to build and create for myself.

One of those ideas eventually became Origami Vault.

---

## The Flight

Around 2018, while working as a Solution Consultant and returning home from a customer visit, I was sitting on a flight with my mind wandering.

I started thinking about creativity, art, and the ways human knowledge is preserved.

At some point, I came across something related to dancing. That led me to wonder:

*"How do people preserve knowledge about dance?"*

We can record dancers performing, but how do we store the actual knowledge behind the movements?

Could dance moves be represented as structured information?

Could they be organized into a database?

Could computers understand and preserve that knowledge in the same way they preserve text, images, or software?

Today we have technologies such as motion capture, but at the time I was thinking about a more fundamental question:

**How do we preserve knowledge about an art form?**

That question stayed with me.

As I thought about dance, my mind began moving toward other forms of artistic expression.

And eventually, I arrived at origami.

---

## Why Origami?

I have never considered myself an origami expert.

In fact, I have only attempted a few origami models throughout my life.

What fascinated me was not just the art itself, but the challenge of preserving and representing the knowledge behind it.

I started asking myself questions:

- Is there a place where all origami models could be stored?
- Is there a way to preserve folding instructions digitally?
- Could creators share and preserve their designs in a structured way?
- Could software understand origami instructions?
- Could a computer one day visualize the folding process?
- Could someone learn an origami model through an interactive 3D experience?

As those questions accumulated, a bigger idea began to emerge.

What if there was a universal library of origami knowledge?

A place where creators, hobbyists, educators, artists, and researchers could share, discover, preserve, and expand the art of paper folding.

A place where the final model mattered, but where the folding knowledge itself was preserved as something valuable.

That idea became the first seed of what would eventually become Origami Vault.

---

## Why It Didn't Happen Then

At the time, the idea felt impossible.

The more I thought about it, the more complicated it seemed.

I imagined the mathematics involved in simulating paper folds.

I thought about 3D graphics, geometry, databases, software architecture, and all the challenges required to represent origami digitally.

The project felt far beyond my abilities.

So I did what many people do with big dreams.

I set it aside.

Life moved on.

Work became busy.

Other interests took priority.

The dream remained in the background for years.

But it never completely disappeared.

---

## Why It Is Happening Now

Years later, the world changed.

Advances in artificial intelligence began transforming the way people learn, research, and create software.

Suddenly, many of the barriers that had once made the project feel impossible no longer seemed quite as intimidating.

I do not see AI as a replacement for creativity, vision, or human decision-making.

Rather, I see AI as a powerful tool.

A tool that can help organize ideas.

A tool that can assist with research.

A tool that can accelerate learning.

A tool that can help write documentation, explore concepts, and assist with programming when needed.

The vision remains human.

The purpose remains human.

The decisions remain human.

But modern tools have made it possible for a single person to pursue ideas that previously might have required an entire team.

For the first time, Origami Vault felt achievable.

---

## The Bigger Question

Over time, I realized that this project was never only about origami.

The deeper question was always:

**How do we preserve human creativity?**

How do we capture knowledge in a way that can survive generations?

How do we represent artistic processes so they can be learned, shared, improved, and built upon?

Origami became the medium through which I chose to explore that question.

What began as an idea for a database of origami models evolved into a vision for preserving and advancing origami knowledge itself.

Not only for people today, but for future generations and perhaps even future technologies.

---

## Looking Forward

Today, Origami Vault is still at the beginning of its journey.

My hope is that it becomes a place where:

- Beginners learn their first folds.
- Artists share original creations.
- Educators find teaching resources.
- Researchers explore the science and mathematics of folding.
- Developers build new tools and experiences.
- Knowledge that might otherwise be lost is preserved.

Perhaps one day it will contribute to new standards for representing origami digitally.

Perhaps it will enable simulations, visualizations, educational tools, 3D models, fabrication workflows, or technologies that do not yet exist.

Or perhaps it will simply help someone discover the joy of folding a piece of paper into something beautiful.

Any of those outcomes would make me proud.

---

## A Personal Note

Origami Vault is, in many ways, the result of a question that followed me for years.

A question about creativity, knowledge, and preservation.

The project may evolve in ways I cannot predict.

The technology will certainly change.

But the mission remains the same:

To preserve, share, and advance the knowledge and beauty of origami for future generations.

If this project helps even a small number of people learn, create, teach, discover, or be inspired, then it will have been worth building.
